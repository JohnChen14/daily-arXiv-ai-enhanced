### [1] [Chiral Electromagnetic Surface Waves on Chern-Simons Interfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21577)
*Nemanja Kaloper*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: Maxwell theory with a codimension-1 Chern-Simons interface supports a gapless, chiral surface photon mode localized on the interface, with linear dispersion and frequency-independent refractive index, even in vacuum without material or geometric confinement.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To demonstrate that chiral electromagnetic surface waves can exist purely due to a Chern-Simons interface in vacuum electrodynamics, without relying on ambient material response or geometric confinement.

Method: Solving the exact boundary value problem for Maxwell theory with a codimension-1 Chern-Simons interface, analyzing helicity-dependent boundary conditions.

Result: The Chern-Simons interaction acts oppositely on the two helicities along the interface, producing one normalizable, gapless chiral surface photon with linear dispersion and frequency-independent index of refraction determined by the Chern-Simons coefficient.

Conclusion: A Chern-Simons interface alone can support chiral surface photons without any bulk material or confinement, revealing a fundamentally new mechanism for surface wave generation.

Abstract: We show that Maxwell theory with a codimension-$1$ Chern-Simons interface supports chiral electromagnetic surface waves on the interface, even when the bulk theory on both sides is conventional vacuum electrodynamics in infinite space. Solving the exact boundary value problem we find that the Chern-Simons interaction acts with opposite sign on the two helicities oriented along the interface, giving rise to one normalizable mode localized on the interface. This mode is a gapless chiral surface photon with linear dispersion and a frequency-independent index of refraction set by the Chern-Simons coefficient. This mode exists despite the absence of ambient material response or geometric confinement.

### [2] [Cosmological Collider in the Grassmannian](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21581)
*Mattia Arundine,Guilherme L. Pimentel*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: The paper computes four-point wavefunction coefficients for conformally coupled scalars exchanging a particle of general mass and spin, using the cosmological Grassmannian to express the correlator in closed form via hypergeometric functions and Legendre polynomials, simplifying the standard approach in momentum space.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Computing four-point wavefunction coefficients for external conformally coupled scalars exchanging a particle of general mass and spin is a central challenge in the cosmological bootstrap, as much of the phenomenology of cosmological collider physics in the near-de Sitter limit follows from this function.

Method: The authors use the cosmological Grassmannian to write the bootstrap differential equation using Plücker coordinates and the basis of Mandelstam invariants. The s-channel correlator is expressed as a hypergeometric function of the S Mandelstam, with spin information encoded in an overall Legendre polynomial factor depending on the other Mandelstams. Boundary conditions are fixed by requiring absence of unphysical singularities and matching to kinematic limits of momentum-space wavefunctions.

Result: The correlator can be written in closed form using hypergeometric functions and Legendre polynomials in Grassmannian space, yielding much simpler expressions than their momentum-space counterparts.

Conclusion: The Grassmannian provides a useful kinematic space for cosmology, significantly simplifying the computation of cosmological correlators compared to traditional momentum-space approaches.

Abstract: We revisit the computation of four-point wavefunction coefficients for external conformally coupled scalars exchanging a particle of general mass and spin. Much of the phenomenology of cosmological collider physics in the near-de Sitter limit follows from this function. Computing it in detail is a central challenge in the cosmological bootstrap. Using the cosmological Grassmannian, we write this correlator in closed form using hypergeometric functions and Legendre polynomials. We achieve this by writing the standard bootstrap differential equation using the Plücker coordinates of the Grassmannian, and using the basis of Mandelstam invariants. The correlator in the s-channel can be written in terms of a hypergeometric function of the S Mandelstam, while the spin information appears as an overall Legendre polynomial factor that also depends on the other Mandelstams. We fix the boundary conditions by first demanding the absence of unphysical singularities, and by further matching to kinematic limits of the momentum-space wavefunction. Our formulae in Grassmannian space are much simpler than their counterparts in momentum space, demonstrating another useful application of the Grassmannian as a kinematic space for cosmology.

### [4] [The Mass Gap Approach to QCD. II. The non-perturbative renormalization program for the massive gluon fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21547)
*V. Gogokhia,G. G. Barnafoldi*

Main category: hep-ph

TL;DR: A non-perturbative multiplicative renormalization program for massive gluon fields is developed within the mass gap approach to QCD, demonstrating dynamic gluon mass acquisition, confinement of massive gluon states (not appearing as mass-shell objects), and consistency with the free massless gluon propagator limit.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To develop a non-perturbative renormalization program for massive gluon fields based on new insights into the true dynamical and gauge structure of the QCD ground state, and to resolve the inconsistency of the canonical gauge in QCD.

Method: Non-perturbative multiplicative renormalization within the mass gap approach to QCD; full analysis of the massive gluon propagator including asymptotic properties, mass-shell structure, and Euclidean metric expressions for lattice simulations.

Result: Gluons acquire mass dynamically; massive gluons are not mass-shell objects and thus are confined (cannot appear in the physical spectrum, only in vacuum or inside hadrons); the massive solution correctly limits to the free massless gluon propagator when the pole mass is set to zero.

Conclusion: The approach provides a consistent non-perturbative framework for massive gluons in QCD, fixing canonical gauge inconsistencies and ensuring gluon confinement while maintaining the correct perturbative limit.

Abstract: We present a non-perturbative multiplicative renormalization program for the massive gluon fields. This has been done within the previously formulated the mass gap approach to QCD. It is based on a new insights into its ground state true dynamical and gauge structures. Our approach makes it possible for gluons to acquire mass dynamically. The corresponding full gluon propagator has been investigated in full details. Its asymptotic properties have been analysed, including the perturbation theory limit. The peculiarities of the mass-shell structure of the full massive gluon propagator has been discussed. The inconsistency of the canonical gauge in QCD is fixed. Our approach does not allow the massive gluons to be the mass-shell objects. This prevents them to appear in the physical spectrum (confinement of massive gluon states). The massive gluons may exist in the vacuum or inside hadrons only. Expressions in Euclidean metric for the lattice simulations are also present. We have also shown that the massive solution has a correct limit to the free massless gluon propagator, when the exactly defined gluon pole mass is to be formally put zero.

### [5] [Echoes of Nucleon Decay from Long-Lived Particles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21583)
*Patrick Adolf,Chandan Hati,Martin Hirsch,Volodymyr Takhistov*

Main category: hep-ph

TL;DR: This paper proposes new nucleon decay observables involving long-lived particles (LLPs) that produce spatially separated but temporally correlated "echo" vertices, which are missed by conventional prompt searches. Focusing on vector LLPs, the authors construct effective operators and UV realizations, showing that Super-Kamiokande, Hyper-Kamiokande and JUNO can achieve geometric acceptances near 80% across a broad range of LLP decay lengths.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Nucleon decay searches are sensitive probes of baryon number violation and beyond-Standard Model physics, but conventional prompt searches may miss signatures involving long-lived particles that produce spatially separated but temporally correlated "echo" vertices.

Method: The authors construct effective operators and ultraviolet realizations for vector LLP-mediated nucleon decay, and analyze the geometric acceptance of Super-Kamiokande, Hyper-Kamiokande and JUNO for these echo signatures.

Result: The proposed detectors can achieve geometric acceptances approaching 80% over a broad range of LLP decay lengths for the echo signatures.

Conclusion: Echo signatures from long-lived particles could provide a new class of nucleon decay observables beyond conventional prompt searches, and could arise from any visibly decaying LLP.

Abstract: Nucleon decay searches provide uniquely sensitive probes of baryon number violation and physics beyond the Standard Model. We propose a new class of nucleon decay observables involving long-lived particles (LLPs), characterized by spatially separated but temporally correlated "echo" vertices not captured by conventional prompt searches. Focusing on vector LLPs, we construct effective operators and ultraviolet realizations, and show that Super-Kamiokande, Hyper-Kamiokande and JUNO can achieve geometric acceptances approaching 80% over a broad range of LLP decay lengths. Echo signatures could in principle arise from any visibly decaying LLP.

### [6] [Precision physics at the muon collider: $m_W$ and CKM matrix elements](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21590)
*Ludovica Aperio Bella,Roberto Franceschini,Federico Meloni,Xing Wang*

Main category: hep-ph

TL;DR: This paper investigates the potential of a 10 TeV lepton collider for precision measurements of the W boson mass and CKM matrix elements, finding that hadronic channels (especially via γW→W) can improve upon current measurements, motivating detector development for high-precision hadronic energy measurements and flavor tagging.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To assess whether a 10 TeV lepton collider can improve precision measurements of the W boson mass and CKM matrix elements beyond current capabilities, given the limitations of hadron colliders and low-energy extractions.

Method: The study examines various W boson production mechanisms at 10 TeV lepton colliders, focusing on the dominant effective γW→W process viable at both opposite-sign and same-sign colliders. It evaluates both leptonic and hadronic W decay channels for their precision potential.

Result: The leptonic W decay channel is non-competitive due to insufficient rate. The hadronic channel can improve upon the current ~10 MeV precision from hadron colliders. The method can also yield CKM matrix element determinations, especially Vcb, surpassing current precision by avoiding low-energy hadronic matrix element uncertainties.

Conclusion: A 10 TeV lepton collider offers promising precision for W boson mass and CKM measurements via hadronic channels, motivating detector R&D for high-precision hadronic energy measurements and flavor tagging.

Abstract: We examine the potential for a 10~TeV lepton collider to carry out precision measurements of the W boson mass and W boson couplings strength, i.e. the CKM matrix elements. We consider the several W boson production mechanisms and focus on the most copious at 10~TeV, that is effective $γW \to W$, a process viable at both opposite sign and same-sign leptonic colliders. We find that the leptonic W decay channel can hardly be competitive with present determinations, due to lack of rate. The hadronic channel has potential to improve over the current $\simeq$10~MeV from measurements at hadron colliders, motivating detector developments towards high-precision hadronic energy measurements. We find that the precision understanding of the detector response to hadrons can also lead to a determination of the CKM matrix elements. We expect determination of CKM matrix elements surpassing by far the present precision for couplings involving heavy quarks, notably $V_{cb}$, avoiding the present bottle-necks due to poor knowledge of hadronic matrix elements needed in low energy extractions of CKM matrix elements. Our findings motivate detector developments towards high-precision hadronic energy measurements and flavor tagging.

</details>
