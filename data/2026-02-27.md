### [1] [NASDUCK': Laboratory Limits on Ultralight Dark-Photon Dark Matter with Null-Axis Magnetometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22308)

Main category: hep-ph

TL;DR: Search for dark-photon dark matter using three-axis magnetometer in shielded room, setting new laboratory limits on kinetic mixing parameter ε, improving previous bounds by up to 3 orders of magnitude.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Dark photon is a well-motivated ultralight dark-matter candidate that may couple to Standard Model through kinetic mixing, requiring experimental searches in relevant mass ranges.

Method: Used three-axis magnetometer inside large conductive shielded room to search for dark-photon dark matter in mass range 4×10⁻¹²-2×10⁻⁹ eV (1-500 kHz). Employed geometry-defined null response along one axis as noise reference and subtraction procedure to reduce noise floor.

Abstract: The dark photon is a well-motivated ultralight dark-matter candidate that may couple to the Standard Model through kinetic mixing. We search for dark-photon dark matter in the mass range $m_{A'}c^2 = 4\times10^{-12}$-$2\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm{eV}$ (1-500 kHz) using a three-axis magnetometer inside a large conductive shielded room. Our search exploits a geometry-defined null response along one axis as a noise reference; a subtraction procedure reduces the noise floor and improves sensitivity. These results establish the strongest laboratory constraints in this mass range and illustrate how null-axis magnetometry can broaden terrestrial searches for ultralight vector dark matter.

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To clarify the origin of Nambu-Goldstone bosons when both anomalous and non-anomalous global symmetries are spontaneously broken simultaneously, particularly addressing the Majoron's symmetry origin and resolving associated domain wall problems.

Method: Theoretical analysis of spontaneous symmetry breaking patterns when anomalous and non-anomalous symmetries are broken together, examining the resulting Nambu-Goldstone boson identification and implications for effective couplings.

Result: The Nambu-Goldstone boson corresponds to the non-anomalous symmetry rather than the anomalous one, meaning the Majoron originates from B-L symmetry rather than L symmetry, naturally resolving domain wall problems and allowing detection through axion-like couplings.

Conclusion: Effective couplings of the form a F ̃F do not uniquely indicate anomalous origins, enabling detection of non-anomalous Nambu-Goldstone bosons like the Majoron in axion searches, potentially providing evidence for new charged fermions.

Abstract: We show that when an anomalous and a non-anomalous global symmetries are spontaneously and simultaneously broken, the resulting Nambu--Goldstone boson is associated with the non-anomalous one. Applied to the Majoron, this implies that its underlying symmetry is $B-L$ rather than $L$, naturally resolving the domain wall problem. This result further demonstrates that effective couplings of the form $a \, F \, \tilde{F}$ do not uniquely indicate an anomalous origin, allowing the detection of non-anomalous Nambu--Goldstone bosons, such as the Majoron, in searches for axion--gauge boson interactions, potentially serving as evidence for the existence of new charged fermions.

  <summary>Details</summary>

Method: Derivation of sum rule using U-spin symmetry in the Standard Model framework, considering Cabibbo-favored (CF), doubly Cabibbo-suppressed (DCS), and singly Cabibbo-suppressed (SCS) decays with CKM factors removed, maintaining validity to second order in U-spin breaking.

Result: The sum rule (CF + DCS CKM-free rates)/(SCS CKM-free rates) = 1 is well satisfied by available experimental data for all tested systems, confirming theoretical prediction; unmeasured rates predicted using this relation.

Conclusion: The derived U-spin sum rule provides a powerful tool for testing Standard Model consistency in charm decays, predicting missing decay rates, and potentially revealing new physics through deviations from the established relation.

Abstract: We show that within the Standard Model any system of hadronic weak charm decays related by $U$-spin satisfies the following rate sum rule: (sum of CF and DCS CKM-free rates) divided by (sum of SCS CKM-free rates) = 1, which holds up to second order in $U$-spin breaking. We test this sum rule against available data and find that it is well satisfied in all cases. For systems in which some decay rates have not yet been measured, we use this sum rule to predict the missing rates.

  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The motivation is to systematically explore asymmetric orbifolds as examples of non-geometric string compactifications, which represent an intriguing corner of the string landscape. Method: The study employs two complementary approaches: duality twists and lattice methods. Conclusion: The dissertation provides a comprehensive study of freely acting asymmetric orbifolds in type IIB string theory, demonstrating their utility as controlled non-geometric compactifications. Abstract: In this dissertation, we study various aspects of type IIB string theory compactified on freely acting orbifolds. We focus particularly on asymmetric orbifolds, which are examples of non-geometric string compactifications and constitute an intriguing corner in the string landscape. First, we discuss the general construction of freely acting (asymmetric) orbifolds, focusing on the closed string sector. Moreover, by constructing dual orbifold pairs, we argue that no quantum corrections to the metric on the vector multiplet moduli space arise. Then, we discuss aspects of the swampland program in the context of freely acting asymmetric orbifolds and we verify that the swampland distance conjecture is valid in the non-geometric compactifications of string theory studied in this thesis. Finally, we focus on the D-brane spectrum of freely acting orbifolds and we determine the conditions under which the various D-branes survive the orbifolding.
