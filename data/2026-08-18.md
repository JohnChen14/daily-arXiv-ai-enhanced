### [1] [Amplified Isospin Breaking from Coupled-Channel Dynamics Near Threshold](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16266)
*Xiang-Kun Dong,Feng-Kun Guo,Christoph Hanhart,Teng Ji,Ulf-G. Meißner*

Main category: hep-ph

TL;DR: This paper identifies the dynamical conditions for amplified isospin breaking in near-threshold states, showing that threshold proximity alone is insufficient—sizable interaction in the companion isospin channel is required. Applied to the X(3872), the observed large isospin breaking implies a nearby partner pole W_c1, which does not evolve into the pure I=1 eigenstate in the isospin limit but connects to a distant shadow pole.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To understand the origin of amplified isospin breaking in near-threshold states, and to clarify whether threshold proximity alone can explain the phenomenon or whether additional dynamical conditions are needed. Also aims to resolve the interpretation of the X(3872)'s large isospin breaking.

Method: Uses a pole-residue-based measure defined directly from pole couplings, rather than decay observables (which are affected by different final-state kinematics), to analyze isospin breaking. Applies this framework to the X(3872) and traces the evolution of poles toward the isospin limit.

Result: Threshold proximity of the pole alone is insufficient for large isospin breaking—sizable interaction in the companion isospin channel is required. For the X(3872), this points to a nearby partner pole W_c1. The W_c1 pole affects line shapes near the charged threshold in the physical case but does not evolve into the pure I=1 eigenstate in the isospin limit; that eigenstate connects to a more distant shadow pole.

Conclusion: Large isospin breaking in near-threshold states is driven by dynamics in the companion isospin channel, not just threshold proximity. The X(3872) results support the existence of a nearby W_c1 partner pole, and the pole structure in the isospin limit reveals a continuous connection to a shadow pole rather than a simple pure isospin eigenstate.

Abstract: We identify the dynamical origin of amplified isospin breaking in near-threshold states. Using a pole-residue-based measure defined directly from the pole couplings, rather than from decay observables that are also affected by different final-state kinematics, we show that threshold proximity of the pole alone is insufficient: large isospin breaking requires a sizable interaction in the companion isospin channel. Applied to the $X(3872)$, the observed large isospin breaking points to a nearby partner pole, $W_{c1}$, as predicted in previous studies. In addition, we find that the $W_{c1}$ pole, which predominantly affects the line shapes near the charged threshold in the physical case, does not evolve into the pure $I=1$ eigenstate in the isospin limit; instead, that eigenstate is continuously connected to a more distant shadow pole.

### [2] [Exotics with gluonic excitations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16784)
*Jozef J. Dudek*

Main category: hep-ph

TL;DR: This chapter reviews the theoretical understanding of gluonic hadrons—glueballs (hadrons made purely of gluons) and hybrids (hadrons with an excited gluonic field coupled to quarks)—and the experimental challenges in identifying these exotic states within the hadron spectrum.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The strong self-interaction of gluons in quantum chromodynamics suggests the existence of hadrons where gluons play an essential structural role, namely glueballs and hybrids. However, definitively identifying these states in the experimental hadron spectrum remains a significant challenge, motivating a review of current theoretical understanding.

Method: The chapter provides a contemporary theoretical synthesis of the nature of glueballs and hybrids based on quantum chromodynamics, and discusses the obstacles associated with unambiguously distinguishing these gluonic states from conventional quark-based hadrons in experimental data.

Result: The chapter outlines the current theoretical framework for gluonic hadrons and highlights the difficulties in definitively identifying glueballs and hybrids within the observed experimental hadron spectrum.

Conclusion: Gluonic hadrons (glueballs and hybrids) are theoretically motivated by the strong self-interaction of gluons in QCD, but their definitive experimental identification remains an open and challenging problem.

Abstract: That the gluons of quantum chromodynamics self-interact with a strong coupling suggests the possibility of hadrons in which the gluonic field plays an essential role: Glueballs in which gluons stick together to produce a hadron in which no quarks are required, and hybrids in which an excited gluonic field couples to quarks. This chapter presents our contemporary theoretical understanding of such states, and the challenges associated with definitively identifying their presence within the experimental spectrum of hadrons.

</details>

### [3] [Effective field equations with stochastic initial conditions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16486)
*Ian G. Moss*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: The paper discusses extending the Truncated Wigner technique — a numerical method for tracking quantum field expectation values from non-equilibrium states — into a fully fledged effective field equation approach that incorporates corrections from higher loops and higher momenta, improving upon its current status as an approximate method subject to quantum loop corrections.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: The Truncated Wigner technique can track non-perturbative quantum phenomena (e.g., false vacuum decay) in real time with promising results, but it is an approximate method that neglects quantum loop corrections. There is a need to extend it into a more complete effective field equation framework.

Method: The paper discusses the extension of the Truncated Wigner approach into a fully fledged effective field equation methodology, including systematic corrections from higher loops and higher momenta.

Result: The paper presents a framework for extending Truncated Wigner to include higher-loop and higher-momentum corrections, addressing the limitations of the original approximate method.

Conclusion: The Truncated Wigner technique can be systematically improved into a full effective field equation approach that incorporates higher-loop and higher-momentum quantum corrections, enabling more accurate tracking of non-perturbative quantum phenomena.

Abstract: The evolution of quantum field expectation values from an initial non-equilibrium quantum state can be followed numerically using the Truncated Wigner technique. It can track non-perturbative quantum phenomena, such as false vacuum decay, in real time with promising results. Nevertheless, it is an approximate method subject to quantum loop corrections. The extension of Truncated Wigner into a fully fledged effective field equation approach is discussed here, including corrections from higher loops and higher momenta.

</details>


### [4] [Flat space limits of symmetric Taub-NUT instantons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16487)
*Matthew Dales*

Main category: hep-th

TL;DR: The paper constructs U(1)-symmetric SU(2) instantons on Taub-NUT space with specific instanton numbers and magnetic charges (k,m) = (2,0), (1,1), and (2,-1) using the bow construction, and shows how these recover flat-space instantons and calorons in various limits, including newly discovered Taub-NUT instantons that do not limit to either.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: To extend the understanding of instantons on curved manifolds, specifically Taub-NUT space, by constructing explicit examples via the bow construction and relating them to known instanton solutions on flat space and calorons.

Method: The bow construction is used, which is a Nahm-like nonlinear transformation that identifies instantons with bow data satisfying an integrable ODE and algebraic relations on an interval.

Result: Successfully constructed U(1)-symmetric SU(2) instantons on Taub-NUT for the specified charges (k,m), recovered the corresponding flat R^4 instantons and calorons in limiting cases, and demonstrated the existence of Taub-NUT instantons that have no such limits.

Conclusion: The bow construction effectively generates instantons on Taub-NUT space, and there exist Taub-NUT instantons that are genuinely distinct from both flat-space instantons and calorons, highlighting the richer instanton moduli space on curved manifolds.

Abstract: We construct $U(1)$-symmetric $SU(2)$ instantons on the Taub-NUT space with generic holonomy, and instanton number and magnetic charge $(k,m)=(2,0)$, $(1,1)$, and $(2,-1)$. We construct these instantons via the bow construction, a Nahm-like non-linear transformation which identifies them with a set of bow data solving an integrable ODE and a series of algebraic relations on an interval. We show how the bow data recovers the data for all corresponding $U(1)$-symmetric instantons on flat $\mathbb{R}^4$ and calorons on $\mathbb{R}^3\times S^1$ in various limits, and we demonstrate the existence of Taub-NUT instantons that do not limit to $\mathbb{R}^4$ instantons nor calorons.

</details>
